## 1. Define Your Target Audience

Before starting any recruitment effort, it's crucial to define who you want to target. A graduate program may consist of multiple student types, such as:

- Traditional students (current and recent undergraduates)
- Professionals looking for career advancement (mid-career individuals)
- International students
- Non-traditional students (those returning after a gap, or those balancing work and study)

Action Steps:

- Consider how each group can most effectively be reached.
- Review current enrollment data to understand where your students are coming from. Feeder schools for each program are available on the [Grad Studies dashboard](https://data.csueastbay.edu/#/enrollment/majors/graduate) on Pioneer Insights under the "Enrollment by Origin Institute tab."


> **note, August 2026.** Direct email through BaySync or Salesforce, Massmail, A-frames, flyers, and the LED screens reach people already enrolled at East Bay. The FAST programs page addresses juniors and transfer students holding 70 to 90 units, the one segment with a page written for it. International applicants route through the International Admissions Office, and Continuing Education carries the self-support and Open University paths. Mid-career professionals and students returning after a gap have no page or channel addressed to them. Feeder-school students appear in the Pioneer Insights origin-institute data and in no outreach channel.

## 2. Strengthen Online Presence

A robust online presence is critical in today's digital-first world. Consider how you might use your program website, social media, and email messaging to reach prospective students.

Action Steps:

- Website Optimization:
  - Ensure your program's website is user-friendly, contains all necessary information (admission requirements, deadlines, etc.), and has a clear call-to-action for inquiries (e.g., "[Request More Info](https://www.csueastbay.edu/futurestudents/request-info.html)" button and an "Apply Here" button.).
  - Include student testimonials, faculty highlights, and success stories.
- Search Engine Optimization and Content Strategy:
  - Optimize your website for relevant search terms (e.g., "online MBA program" or "graduate degree in X") to capture organic traffic.
  - Publish relevant blog posts, research highlights, and faculty Q&A to build organic interest.
- Social Media Marketing:
  - Create a presence on platforms like LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and Twitter.
  - Share relevant content such as program details, alumni stories, student life, and faculty interviews.
  - Engage with followers by responding to comments and messages.

Best Practice: Student Outreach suggested posting to social media accounts 2-3 times a week. Since this may be very time-intensive, consider submitting posts for your college's social media accounts or submitting to Graduate Studies to post on our accounts. Also, ask your current students to post about your program and their own experiences as a graduate student on their own accounts. Review the university's [digital marketing campaign](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1ymIy8OEcxhVBCpKeQR2QmCLozCPCAuWv?usp=sharing) to align your outreach with university-wide marketing efforts and avoid redundant work.


> **note, August 2026.** Social media runs through the Office of Graduate Studies Instagram and LinkedIn accounts and the university accounts. The Graduate Studies site carries the program directory, the coordinator directory, the Request for Information form, the FAST page, the graduate student handbook, and the financial resources page. Program pages themselves are maintained department by department, and the prospective students page links out to each college's own admissions pages. Search terms, testimonials, and a consistent call to action have no owner across those pages.

## 3. Host Virtual Information Sessions and Webinars

Virtual information sessions are highly effective in engaging prospective students and answering their questions. They provide a personalized touch and a platform to highlight your program's unique selling points.

Action Steps:

- Schedule Regular Information Sessions:
  - Host monthly or twice a semester virtual sessions where faculty and staff discuss program details, answer questions, and provide guidance on application procedures.
  - Encourage prospective students to sign up via an easy-to-use registration page.
- Leverage Alumni and Current Students:
  - Invite alumni and current students to speak about their experience in the program. Hearing real-life success stories helps build trust and enthusiasm.
- Offer Special Webinars on Specific Topics:
  - For example, a webinar on "Navigating the Application Process" or "How Our MBA Program Prepares You for a Career in Tech."
- Promote Information Sessions:
  - Use email marketing and social media to promote upcoming sessions.
  - Cross-promote via university-wide channels like student organizations, faculty newsletters, and department pages.

Best Practice: Despite little to no external marketing of information sessions, programs who hold them state that they get a lot of interest. Ensure that the sign-up link, contact information, and session date and time are all clear and easy to find on your program website. The MBA program suggests guiding participants through your website rather than presenting a PowerPoint presentation allowing them to see where useful resources are located more easily. The Office of Graduate Studies will be creating an index of information sessions on the [Grad Studies site](https://www.csueastbay.edu/graduate-studies/index.html), and will post about them on Grad Studies social media channels.

## 4. Partner with External Organizations and Networks

Building partnerships with professional associations, alumni networks, and student groups can extend your reach to a broader audience.

Action Steps:

- Partner with Industry Associations:
  - Consider contacting organizations like the National Teacher Association, industry groups, or professional development bodies. Offer exclusive webinars or workshops to members of these organizations.
- Leverage Alumni Networks:
  - Encourage alumni to promote the program via social media, speaking at info sessions, or referring prospective students. Our graduate students are particularly interested in their career prospects, so this is very effective advertising.
  - Create an alumni ambassador program to help with outreach efforts.
- Engage Industry Advisory Board
  - While we often consider Industry Advisory Boards to be possible employers of our graduates, they can also help to recruit for our graduate programs by posting on their internal communication channels, advertising information sessions and webinars.

Best Practice: Hosting Collaborative information sessions, webinars, or panels can help you reach new audiences. For example, if you're offering a construction management program, partner with a professional association to co-host a webinar on the "Future of Construction in California" and include a segment about your program. Ensure that the event provides value to the external organization as well. For instance, you can offer the organization access to your program's resources, exposure to your faculty, or training materials that help their members.

## 5. Nurture Leads and Maintain Relationships

It's not enough to just generate leads — they need to be nurtured and guided through the application process. A recent report stated that the overall yield across all programs at East Bay was just 9%.

Action Steps:

- Implement a Lead Nurturing System:
  - Use an email marketing platform to send automated follow-up emails to prospective students who have shown interest. For example, after they register for an information session or download a brochure, send them tailored emails with further details on admissions, deadlines, etc.
- Personalized Outreach:
  - Assign staff or faculty members to personally reach out to highly engaged leads. A personalized email or phone call can make a huge difference.
- Application Assistance:
  - Create a step-by-step guide or checklist on the application process and share it with leads. Offer webinars on how to submit strong application materials (essays, letters of recommendation, etc.).

Best Practice: Admissions is building a new Graduate Admissions Portal, which will provide personalized information for students who have completed a Request for Information form or begun an application in Cal State Apply. Admissions will also send personalized messages to these students to encourage them to complete an application or accept an offer of admission. Individual departments can also send messages to their leads using the Salesforce Marketing Cloud. You may contact ITS for more information.

The former MBA program coordinator related that he would call each participant who had attended an information session and discuss their particular situation and how an East Bay degree could help them. He stated that this resulted in a yield of 30% for his program. This really shows how a personal touch can make a big difference.


> **note, August 2026.** The Request for Information form is the visible entry point, and what follows it is not visible from outside. Salesforce appears in the inventory as a send mechanism for direct email to enrolled students. Follow-up after an event, personal outreach to engaged leads, and application assistance beyond the posted Cal State Apply materials are unstaffed.

## 6. Track Metrics and Optimize

Evaluate the effectiveness of each recruitment tactic and adjust your strategy accordingly.

Action Steps:

- Measure Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):
  - Track metrics such as webinar attendance, lead conversion rates, and application submission rates.
  - Monitor which outreach channels are producing the most qualified leads (e.g., email vs. social media). Can be easily done when students pre-register for events like information sessions. [Example](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qCkax0AOvFc0oEP5K1vM8DFN7sKbQZBxwSJBdCgWjG0/edit?usp=sharing).
- Analyze the Funnel:
  - Evaluate where prospective students drop off in the recruitment process. For example, if many people register for webinars but don't apply, there may be a gap in follow-up or information clarity.
- Solicit Feedback:
  - After information sessions or webinars, ask attendees for feedback on what they found helpful and what could be improved. This feedback can inform future sessions.


> **note, August 2026.** Nothing visible attributes a lead to a source. Pre-registration is already in use for the connections event and the graduate school fair, and the ambassador interest survey runs through a Google form, so collection points exist without attribution running through them.

## 7. Provide Financial and Career Support Information

Cost is a significant factor in graduate school decision-making. Highlighting scholarships, financial aid options, and career services can make a big difference.

Action Steps:

- Scholarships and Financial Aid:
  - Clearly communicate any available scholarships, assistantships, or fellowships and encourage new master's students to apply for Graduate Equity Fellowships as soon as they are accepted.
- Career Services:
  - Promote the university's Career Empowerment Center. Highlight any partnerships with companies, internships, or job placement programs that may be available to students.

> **note, August 2026.** The Graduate Opportunities and Financial Resources page and the Next Step CSU Graduate Career Readiness page both sit in the Graduate Studies navigation, and the Career Empowerment Center presented on application essays at the Fall 2024 workshops. Graduate Equity Fellowships appear in no outreach channel. This material reaches prospective students through the site and through nothing else.
