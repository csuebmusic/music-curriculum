How your schedule comes together

This depends on how you enter, and most of it happens between late May and early July. If you are starting as a first-year student, the CLASS Student Success Center builds your first-semester schedule with the department’s music advisors, and you do not register yourself in the first year. If you are transferring or already hold a bachelor’s, we schedule an advising session with you to plan your courses, and you register yourself once they are settled. Watch for our email over the summer and pick a time.

Before the semester starts

  • Applied area placement. You need one before starting lessons on your principal instrument or voice, any time up to the end of the first week. A scholarship audition already counts. What to prepare.
  • Ensembles. Auditions and placement happen at the first meeting of each ensemble. Register for the course, then turn up to that first class. Our ensembles.
  • Music orientation. The Department holds it on the Thursday before the first week of classes. You will walk our facilities, rent a locker, and meet your cohort and your advisors.
  • Concert attendance. While you are enrolled in applied lessons you need ten a semester. Any Department event counts as one credit, and performing in one counts as a half. You record your own attendance with OneTap on your phone, and the visitor app is worth installing before your first event.

Read ahead

The Undergraduate Handbook holds the whole degree: applied levels and juries, recital requirements, ensembles, health and safety, and the policies you will want in hand. Student Resources is the shorter route, with advising, roadmaps, and forms alongside. The Music Resource Center and the Music Equipment Office hold the scores, instruments, and gear, and concerts go up on News & Events.
