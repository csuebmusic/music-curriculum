How your schedule comes together

This depends on how you enter, and most of it happens between late May and early July. If you are starting as a first-year student, the CLASS Student Success Center builds your first-semester schedule with the department’s music advisors, and you do not register yourself in the first year. If you are transferring or already hold a bachelor’s, we schedule an advising session with you to plan your courses, and you register yourself once they are settled. Watch for our email over the summer and pick a time.

Before the semester starts

  • Applied area placement. You need one before starting lessons on your principal instrument or voice, any time up to the second week of the semester (if you submitted a scholarship audition, that already counts!) What to prepare.
  • Ensembles. Auditions and placement happen at the first meeting of each ensemble. Register for the course, then come ready to play or sing to that first class. Our ensembles.
  • Music orientation. The Department holds it the day before classes start. You will walk our facilities, rent a locker, and meet your cohort and your advisors.

Read ahead (all of these are on our Student Resources page)

  • The Undergraduate Handbook sets out the degree in full: applied levels and juries, recital requirements, ensembles, health and safety, and the academic policies of the department.
  • The roadmaps lay out the degree term by term, four years or two years for transfer students.
  • The Music Resource Center lends scores, parts, and study materials.
  • The Music Equipment Office lends instruments and recording equipment, and assigns lockers.
  • The concert calendar is on News & Events.

Two other paths worth knowing about now

  • The Single Subject Matter Preparation Certificate in Music is an add-on to the B.A. that prepares you to apply for the California Single Subject Teaching Credential in Music and complete it successfully. Its roadmap shows how the coursework fits alongside the degree. John Eros coordinates music education: john.eros@csueastbay.edu. Reach out to him directly to schedule an advising session.
  • The FAST 4+1 B.A./M.A. gets you both degrees in five years. You apply by January 15 of your junior year, with 60 to 90 units done and a 3.0 in your music courses. You can find out more about it here, and the roadmaps lay out the whole sequence: 4+1 and 2+1 for transfer students.
