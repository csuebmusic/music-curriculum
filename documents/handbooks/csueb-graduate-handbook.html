graduate student handbook

Office of Graduate Studies · California State University, East Bay

welcome

Welcome to graduate study at Cal State East Bay. This handbook covers the university requirements, policies, and procedures that apply to every graduate and post-baccalaureate student here: how admission and enrollment work, what the degree requires, how grades and academic standing are handled, and how the capstone is written, reviewed, and archived.

Your program sets requirements above these, and your graduate program coordinator is the person who knows them. Meet with that coordinator before registering each semester and whenever a question about the degree comes up.

scope of this handbook

The University Catalog is the authoritative source for academic policy and for the requirements of each degree. This handbook supplements it and doesn't replace it. Where the two differ, the catalog governs, and the discrepancy should go to the Office of Graduate Studies so it can be corrected.

You are responsible for your own academic progress: keeping a record of the courses you've taken, knowing which catalog year applies to you, and checking in with your graduate program coordinator.

general information

the office of graduate studies

The Office of Graduate Studies supports prospective and current graduate students across the university, from admissions guidance through research, professional development, and career planning. It administers the fellowships described in section 06, coordinates the Graduate Advisory Council, and runs the format review and archiving process for University Theses.

graduate program coordinators

Each degree program has a faculty graduate program coordinator who supervises the program and advises its students. Arrange your advising appointments with that coordinator. Contacts for every program are on the graduate program coordinators page.

international students

Graduate students on a student visa contact the Center for International Education about visa status, employment eligibility, and any special academic requirements. The International Admissions Office sets separate admission requirements and filing dates.

financial support

File the FAFSA or the California Dream Act Application each year; eligibility for most support depends on having one on file. Tuition and fees are on the cost of attendance page. Grants, loans, work study, and the fellowships the Office of Graduate Studies administers are described on the graduate opportunities and financial resources page.

admission and enrollment

transitory admission

Graduate students are eligible for Intrasystem Concurrent Enrollment, Intrasystem Visitor Enrollment, and cross-registration with the University of California, Berkeley and selected other East Bay colleges. Contact the Office of the Registrar for either intrasystem option.

Intrasystem Concurrent Enrollment lets a student enrolled at any CSU campus attend another campus while still enrolled at the home campus. Intrasystem Visitor Enrollment lets a student of any CSU campus attend another campus as a visiting student for one term. Both require that you have completed at least one term at the home campus as a matriculated student, or will be enrolled concurrently in that status; are or have been enrolled in an authorized graduate program at the home campus; and are in good standing at the last college attended.

returning students

Cal State East Bay students receive an automatic leave of absence for one semester. The leave maintains continuous attendance status, keeps you eligible to register in following semesters, and holds the catalog that determines your degree requirements.

Former students absent for two or more semesters apply for readmission and pay an application fee. Readmission may carry new university or program requirements. Students who left in negative academic standing may need additional coursework before returning, after a mandatory interruption of one or more semesters. Students who were academically disqualified or dismissed file an appeal for reinstatement.

enrollment and degree progress

Graduate students must enroll in courses that satisfy their degree program, master's or credential, to qualify for veterans' benefits or benefits as dependents of disabled or deceased veterans, Department of Homeland Security certification for international students, intercollegiate athletic competition, and most types of financial aid.

Full-time enrollment for graduate students and classified post-baccalaureate students is 8 or more units for financial aid, F-1 and J-1 visas, and veterans or reservists under Chapter 30, 31, 33, 34, 35, or 1606. These classifications apply to fall and spring semesters and to summer session, not to University Extension non-degree programs or shorter sessions including winter. Veterans and eligible dependents consult the Veteran Benefits Coordinator before the first day of classes and submit forms requesting certification of enrollment.

degree requirements

the master's degree

A master's degree requires advancement to candidacy; at least 30 semester units applicable to the degree, with the total for each program given in the catalog; a program of study approved by the department that includes a thesis, project, or comprehensive examination; and a 3.0 grade point average in all units counted toward the degree, with a minimum grade of C in every course. A course counting toward degree requirements with a grade of C− or below must be repeated.

Units carry these limits. All units must be earned within the five calendar years immediately preceding receipt of the degree; outdated units may be accepted for one additional year with approval from the major department and Graduate Studies, and in the sixth year currency in the subject matter must be demonstrated. Courses older than seven years apply only in exceptional circumstances. At least 70 percent of the total units required by the program must be completed in residence as an admitted graduate student, which for a 30-unit program is 21 units. At least half the units must be in stand-alone 600-level courses, and up to half may be in upper-division undergraduate or dual-listed courses. No lower-division units count. No more than 6 units of university thesis, departmental thesis, or project work count. No more than 8 units of independent study count. No units taken CR/NC count unless the course is offered exclusively on that basis, which is mostly fieldwork and internships; this doesn't apply to prerequisite or proficiency coursework.

program requirements

Individual programs may set more stringent requirements, including higher minimum grades. Check the catalog entry for your program and confirm with your graduate program coordinator.

academic course load

A graduate student who wants a master's degree in two years enrolls in about 8 units per semester, 15 units a year in a 30-unit program. Eight units per semester is the minimum for financial aid and reporting purposes. For doctoral course load in Educational Leadership, contact that department.

transferring units

Because at least 70 percent of program units must be completed in residence, transfer credit is limited to the remainder. Units don't count as resident if taken at other schools, as an undergraduate with permission to take graduate courses, while pursuing an additional baccalaureate in unclassified post-baccalaureate status, while enrolled in another graduate degree program, or through Continuing Education including Open University. Special session courses, such as summer, count as resident units. Some programs require more than 70 percent in residence.

To transfer credit, you must have taken the course after earning a bachelor's degree and within the five calendar years immediately preceding receipt of your degree; your department must accept the course as relevant to your program; and the institution must customarily grant the level of credit, graduate or upper division, that you want to receive here.

Cal State East Bay generally doesn't allow credit-by-examination from challenged courses toward master's degree requirements. Departments may establish exceptions, noted in the degree description in the catalog, under which you must pass the challenge exam with a B− or better, no more than 9 semester units of credit-by-examination apply to the degree, and challenged courses don't count toward residency.

graduate certificate programs

Students already enrolled as post-baccalaureates may apply to a graduate certificate program. Students not already enrolled apply to the university as a post-baccalaureate classified student. Applicants meet the minimum requirements for admission to graduate and post-baccalaureate studies and contact the department for additional criteria. Where a degree program in the sponsoring department is appropriate, seeking admission to it lets graduate units count later toward the degree; no more than 9 units, or 30 percent for master's programs over 30 units, taken in classified post-baccalaureate status apply toward a graduate degree.

A certificate program is a coherent set of academic courses narrower in scope than a degree. Each contains a minimum of 12 units, or 15 where the program includes undergraduate courses, which must be upper division. Only one course below the 600 level may be taken CR/NC, and no graduate course may be taken CR/NC unless that is the only grading pattern. At least 75 percent of the courses and all 600-level courses are taken at Cal State East Bay, through University Extension or as a regularly admitted student. Completion is recognized by the certificate itself; there's no notation on the diploma or permanent record, though the courses appear on the record.

change in educational objective

Continuing post-baccalaureate students changing their degree objective or credential program file a Change of Graduate Objective Request with the Office of Graduate Admissions.

doctoral degrees

Cal State East Bay offers a doctoral degree in Educational Leadership. Policies and procedures for continuing student performance and graduation requirements differ from those for master's programs. Students in the Educational Leadership for Social Justice program work closely with their academic advisors; contact the Department of Educational Leadership for requirements and complete program information.

writing proficiency

All students demonstrate competency in writing skills as a graduation requirement. Academic Programs and Services has the current ways to meet it. Some graduate programs require coursework that also fulfills the requirement; students in those programs complete that coursework to earn the degree.

academic policies

grading

The typical grading pattern for graduate courses is A–F, and the use of plus and minus is at each instructor's discretion. Grades lower than C don't satisfy master's degree requirements, and some programs require higher; check with your department. A B−, though described as adequate, generates fewer than 3.0 grade points and must be balanced by a B+ or higher to maintain a 3.0 GPA.

Students can't select CR or NC. Those grades are available only in graduate courses offered exclusively on a credit/no credit basis, typically fieldwork and internships, where CR indicates work of at least B− quality. The same holds for post-baccalaureate 500-level courses. Courses taken CR/NC meet degree requirements only where that is the sole grading pattern.

Grades assigned are presumed correct, and anyone appealing one carries the burden of showing otherwise. To correct an error, an instructor may change a grade with approval from the department chair and college dean. Once graduation is recorded, no grade changes are allowed except to correct an error, which students have one term after the degree is awarded to dispute, or as the result of a grade appeal.

appealing a grade

Students who believe an appropriate grade wasn't assigned start with the instructor, then the chair of the department offering the course, then the dean or associate dean of that college. If those steps don't resolve it, contact the Presidential Appointee to the Grade Appeal and Academic Grievance Committee at 510-885-3716. Students who have applied for graduation notify their graduation evaluator that a question is pending.

The Presidential Appointee assists students in exhausting normal channels and, where needed, in filing a Grade Appeal Petition. Petitions and supporting documentation go to Academic Programs and Services. The committee reviews the documentation and the other parties' responses, and convenes a hearing panel where it finds possible grounds. Under normal circumstances the petition is filed before the end of the semester following the one in which the alleged incident took place. The committee can change or assign a grade after a proper review of the case.

grade point average

Your GPA is the number of grade points earned divided by quality hours, meaning units attempted excluding CR/NC courses. If you repeat a course required in the major, your department has discretion in specific circumstances to exclude the earlier attempt, submitted by the department to the Office of the Registrar at any time while you're enrolled but not after the degree is awarded.

The graduate program coordinator or department chair may also request that grades in courses not counting toward degree requirements be excluded, permitted only where those grades would place you on academic probation. These may include courses from a second baccalaureate program or a different graduate degree program. They may not include courses taken after admission to the current program, courses in the program you later elect not to count, or courses used for a conferred post-baccalaureate degree. Requests go to Academic Programs and Services, which makes the final determination.

below 3.00

If your GPA falls below 3.00, consult your graduate program coordinator or department chair immediately.

registration

Master's students follow the same registration policies as undergraduates; see Registration and Enrollment in the catalog. Thesis and project courses differ: complete a Special Registration Petition for each semester you enroll in one, submitted to the department office before the add/drop deadline, and check on its progress yourself.

Courses that typically require a Special Registration Petition are independent study, individual study, capstone project, departmental thesis, and university thesis.

withdrawal from the university

Students cancelling registration or withdrawing from all classes after enrolling follow the university's official withdrawal procedures. Skipping them can mean an obligation to pay fees, failing grades in all courses, and applying for readmission before enrolling again. Students who fail to fulfill enrollment or matriculation requirements, or otherwise fail to follow academic or university regulations, are subject to administrative action including Administrative Probation or cancelled enrollment.

During the add/drop period, dropping all courses in MyCSUEB requires no withdrawal form. After add/drop ends, from the third through the twelfth week of instruction, complete a withdrawal form, obtain approval signatures from each instructor and department chair, and return it to the Office of the Registrar. A semester you withdraw from doesn't count as an interruption of enrollment for matriculated status. An approved late withdrawal petition doesn't produce a fee refund, and withdrawal doesn't automatically result in one; contact Student Financial Services about refunds.

Financial aid recipients consult a financial aid counselor before withdrawing, since withdrawing during a term or payment period may require returning or repaying funds and may create a debt to the institution.

Matriculated students who don't enroll for two consecutive semesters, not counting summer or winter, and don't enroll in the third, are discontinued and submit a new application for readmission.

declassification

A student may be declassified from a graduate degree or credential program for reasons including unprofessional conduct, failure to maintain professional standards, behavior that interferes with the learning of others, failure to progress toward the degree as set out by university and program policies, failure to complete required components such as fieldwork or practicum courses, failure to meet grade requirements for good standing, or a determination by program faculty that the student can't complete degree requirements at the level expected in the discipline even with a GPA above 3.0.

The major department initiates the request with support from the chair and the college dean or designee. Requests go to Academic Programs and Services for final action and official notification to the student and the Registrar. That office also determines whether the student is academically or administratively disqualified from the university. A declassified student who isn't disqualified and wants to continue applies formally to another graduate program, and can't enroll through the university or Open University in any undergraduate or graduate course in the program they were declassified from. Unless the declassification involved conduct that interferes with campus interactions, declassified students may apply to a new program and be accepted by it. Acceptance must come no later than two semesters after declassification; otherwise the student reapplies to the university.

research with human subjects

The university has policies and procedures protecting human subjects in research, development, and related activities. An Institutional Review Board reviews research protocols to determine whether human subjects would be at risk and to protect their rights and welfare. Protocols are approved before research begins. The Office of Research and Sponsored Programs has the policy document.

the capstone

Every graduate program ends in a thesis, a project, or a comprehensive examination. The quality of the work, expression included, is the major consideration in judging it. Your program specifies which one you complete, and some programs offer a choice.

the three kinds

A University Thesis is a formal presentation of an objective, critical investigation, written in a formal, scholarly manner in standard English. It demonstrates originality, clear purpose, appropriate organization, critical analysis, independent thinking, and accurate and complete documentation, with consistent style, language, and punctuation. Description, cataloging, compilation, and other simple procedures aren't adequate. University Theses follow university formatting guidelines, undergo format review by the Office of Graduate Studies, and are archived in ScholarWorks. University Thesis courses are numbered 691.

A Departmental Thesis matches the University Thesis in objective and content. Its format is specified by your department rather than the university, and it doesn't undergo format review by the Office of Graduate Studies. Departmental Thesis courses are numbered 699.

A Capstone Project is a significant undertaking in a fine or applied art or in a professional field, providing evidence of originality, independent thinking, and appropriate form and organization. You describe the project in a written abstract addressing its significance, objectives, methodology, and conclusions, and in some cases present an oral defense. Capstone Project courses are numbered 693.

Across all three: you must be an admitted graduate student to enroll in a thesis or project course, no more than 6 units count toward the degree, and a student who changes between a thesis program and a project program may count a combined maximum of 6 units of 691, 693, and 699. Each semester you receive a grade of RP, Report in Progress, until the work is finished, at which point you receive a final grade for the entire course. RP units count toward neither the degree nor the GPA. An RP in a thesis course becomes F or NC after five years; an RP in a project course becomes F, with no NC option, after one year.

Questions of content and academic quality are settled by your thesis or project advisor. For a Departmental Thesis or Capstone Project, your program coordinator has the format requirements. If your department doesn't specify a format, following the University Thesis guidelines will produce a document of professional quality.

how to begin

Start with your department. Consult a graduate advisor about your program and its requirements for the capstone, then select a thesis or project advisor whose research interests match yours and with whom you have a good working relationship. That advisor guides you through the selection and definition of a topic and methodology, which is the element that most determines whether the thesis succeeds.

For University and Departmental Theses, form a thesis committee. Guidelines vary by department, so contact yours. The chair of your committee must be a Cal State East Bay faculty member. Capstone Projects may or may not require a committee.

Previously published papers can be used for a thesis if you're first or senior author on them, the publishers give written permission to use them, and you supply an introductory chapter and a concluding chapter connecting the papers together.

Enroll in your department's thesis or project course. For a University Thesis, download the University Thesis Writing Guide and the Thesis Format One-Pager, and settle your topic with departmental guidance and thesis committee approval.

irb approval

Approval of your research protocol by the Institutional Review Board is mandatory where research involves human subjects, and must be obtained before research begins. Any research involving surveys, experiments, questionnaires, interviews, and similar methodology involves human subjects, so theses and projects in the social and behavioral sciences, education, the health fields, and management and administration will probably require review.

timing

Contact the IRB Coordinator early in your planning and submit your completed protocol at least two months before you plan to begin research.

initial format review

Students completing a University Thesis complete a format review through the Office of Graduate Studies. As of Fall 2024 this happens in two stages: an initial application by the end of the tenth week of classes declaring that you intend to submit a completed thesis, and the completed thesis by the final deadline at the end of the fourteenth week.

By the initial format review deadline, submit the Thesis/Capstone Submission Information Form along with your title page, table of contents, and sample first page, plus a sample figure if your thesis will contain figures. Email these to both gradstudies@csueastbay.edu and thesiseditor@csueastbay.edu with "Initial University Thesis Submission" in the subject line.

During the review the Thesis Editor returns the Thesis Checklist and a copy of your thesis with recommended edits. Revise and return it to the Thesis Editor with "Revised Thesis" in the subject line for a final check at this stage.

signature page

Submit the Signature Page unsigned. It's approved for format first, and signatures are collected afterward through AdobeSign.

defense

If your department or committee requires an oral defense of a University Thesis, you must have successfully defended it and made all committee-requested changes before submitting for final format review. Between final format review and electronic library submission, only the formatting changes the Thesis Editor requests may be made; content can't change once the thesis has gone in for final review.

Departments may require an oral defense of a Departmental Thesis or Capstone Project. Contact yours for details.

final format review

By the final format review deadline, submit the completed and defended thesis to thesiseditor@csueastbay.edu and gradstudies@csueastbay.edu with "Final University Thesis Submission" in the subject line. It can go as a single PDF or as two, with the preliminary pages separate from the content pages. Early submissions are appreciated.

Remind your committee members to sign the Signature Page digitally; they were emailed a request when you made your initial submission. If the chair of your committee will be away from campus during your final semester, you're responsible for arranging final approval. The Thesis Editor returns the checklist and recommended edits again, and sends an approval email when the thesis is ready to archive.

the signature page

For University Theses, the Signature Page and the committee approval signatures are handled through AdobeSign. After you submit the Thesis/Capstone Submission Information Form, the Office of Graduate Studies emails an AdobeSign request to your Horizon account. Enter your thesis title in all capitals and delete any unused lines. Once you select Submit, the office routes the page to your committee members, and you receive a copy when they've approved.

Special characters in a title need a workaround. On Windows, use Character Map to find a symbol or accented letter, copy it, and paste it into the AdobeSign field; mathematical equations must be typed in Word and pasted, following Microsoft's instructions. On macOS, hold a key to open the accent menu, or press Control-Command-Space for the Character Viewer; equations must be typed in Pages and pasted, following Apple's instructions.

required elements

A University Thesis contains four mandatory sections, shown below in bold. The rest are optional. Assemble them in this order.

  1. Title page
  2. Copyright page
  3. Abstract
  4. Signature page, completed through AdobeSign
  5. Acknowledgments
  6. Table of contents
  7. Lists of figures, graphs, photos, plates, maps, and tables, if used
  8. Preface
  9. Page 1, the first page of thesis text
  10. Endnotes, if not in text or at the end of chapters
  11. Bibliography
  12. Appendix

Use the title page template in the University Thesis Writing Guide, inserting the same number of blank lines the template indicates. To check alignment, hold your typed title page up to the light against a copy of the template; alignment varies slightly with font.

what faculty do

Faculty members aren't expected to make extensive corrections or revisions. They return draft materials with general instructions and suggestions for bringing the work up to departmental and university standards.

archiving

Theses and projects are archived electronically in ScholarWorks, the CSUEB institutional repository. Submit the final PDF to the Library through University Archives, along with a completed and signed Non-Exclusive Distribution License and Availability Agreement. Email both to archives@csueastbay.edu, including a copy of the signed Signature Page. The Department Chair signature on the agreement is required only for an embargo.

For University Theses, make sure every committee signature has been collected through AdobeSign by the deadline. Once the Library has the thesis, the Office of Graduate Studies notifies the graduation evaluators in the Records Office that it's been accepted.

For Departmental Theses and Capstone Projects, email the Thesis/Capstone Submission Information Form to gradstudies@csueastbay.edu with a subject line of "Departmental Thesis Submission Info Form" or "Capstone Project Submission Form". Ask your advisor or instructor to email the same address with a subject line of "Thesis Approval: Student Name" or "Project Approval: Student Name", including your name, department, and title. For capstone courses, an instructor may send one message listing every student who completed a project.

embargo

Items in ScholarWorks are open access by default, reachable through the repository, the library catalog, and search engines. Restricting access to a deposited item is an embargo, and items may be embargoed for six months, one year, or two years where necessary, as at many institutional repositories. During an embargo the full-text PDF isn't available online, but a record for the work appears in ScholarWorks noting the date the full text becomes available.

Embargoes are rarely necessary, since many publishers allow deposit of pre-submission versions in institutional repositories before publication. You can check a journal's pre-print, post-print, and embargo policies in Open Policy Finder, the Jisc service that replaced Sherpa/RoMEO. Discuss any need for an embargo with your advisor before submitting, and note the decision on the Non-Exclusive Distribution License and Availability Agreement. If you don't restrict access, your advisor doesn't need to sign the form.

bound copies

Some departments require one or two printed and bound copies. Verify this with your thesis chair or project advisor. If a bound copy is required, or you want one for yourself, contract with a binding service directly.

deadlines

Students completing a University Thesis submit for format review by the deadlines for the semester in which they intend to graduate. These are firm: missing them delays graduation. Students completing a Departmental Thesis or Capstone Project don't submit for review by the Office of Graduate Studies, since their department determines the format, but they do submit an electronic copy to the Library for ScholarWorks. Their ScholarWorks deadline is two weeks after the electronic library submission deadline below.

completing your degree

graduate study completion

Students who have finished all units required for the master's degree but are still working on the capstone, whether a thesis, a project, or studying for a comprehensive examination, can keep access to university facilities by registering for XXXX 890 Graduate Study Completion through Continuing Education. Register under your major prefix: a graduate chemistry major registers for CHEM 890. It's a 1-unit course, and the current fee is printed on the registration form.

Registration gives you a valid student ID card, the ability to check out library books, remote access to the library's databases, use of campus computer labs, the ability to keep working on projects in science labs, eligibility to buy a parking permit, and other benefits regularly registered students have. Enrollment also counts as enrollment for students serving as teaching associates.

Pick up a Graduate Study Completion Form in your department or at Continuing Education in SF 102, or download the registration form. You'll need a signature from your major department.

unit credit

XXXX 890 doesn't satisfy any unit or course requirement for the master's degree.

dual and second master's degrees

To pursue two master's degrees at the same time, apply to your primary degree through Cal State Apply and file a Change of Graduate Objective form with the Office of Graduate Admissions for the second. You can request the second objective any time before you graduate under the first. Acceptance to the second program isn't guaranteed, and the decision rests on that program's admission requirements.

To pursue a second master's degree after earning one, apply and gain admission to the department offering it.

Both paths carry the same requirements after acceptance. Fulfill all prerequisites and requirements for the second degree. Complete at least 70 percent of its units in residence after admission to that program, which is 21 units for a 30-unit program. Up to 30 percent of the units from the first degree, 9 units for a 30-unit program, may apply to the second where that program accepts them; where none are accepted, all units for the second degree must be completed. Earn all units within the five calendar years immediately preceding receipt of the degree.

same field

A second degree in the same field, business administration for instance, must be in a different concentration, and every unit in it must come from different courses.

probation, disqualification, and reinstatement

Maintain a 3.00 GPA in all units attempted after admission to your graduate program, prerequisite courses included, to stay in good standing. Below 3.00 you're placed on Academic Probation and consult your graduate advisor before registering for the next semester. You may be academically disqualified if you don't reach a 3.0 GPA during a semester on probation, if you don't meet your department's academic criteria, or, immediately upon notice, if a campus administrator finds behavior so contrary to the standards of the profession you're preparing for as to render you unfit for it.

Administrative Probation applies if you withdraw from all courses for two consecutive semesters or any three semesters, don't progress toward your degree while enrolled, don't comply with academic requirements, or earn only IC, F, WU, or NC grades for two consecutive or any three semesters. Administrative Disqualification follows if you don't meet the conditions for removing Administrative Probation, are placed on it twice for the same reason, or are placed on Academic Probation while on it.

Students on Academic Probation are encouraged to use university services such as advising and tutoring.

Students disqualified academically or administratively may apply for reinstatement to their graduate program through a Petition for Graduate Reinstatement, approved by the graduate program coordinator or department chair and by the Office of Graduate Studies. Reinstatement is granted under one of four conditions.

  • Program of study. Enroll in 6 to 9 units of coursework approved by your graduate coordinator through Open University and earn a 3.3 overall GPA in them. Those courses may count for program credit. Coordinators can ask the Office of Graduate Studies for exceptions to the unit requirement. File the petition after completing the coursework, specifying this option and listing the courses.
  • Grade change. Where an incomplete is resolved or a grade was posted in error, the corrected term GPA may move your standing from disqualification back to alert or good standing. Specify this option and attach a copy of the change of grade form as soon as possible.
  • Extenuating circumstances. An approved retroactive course or semester withdrawal filed after academic standing posts may change the term GPA the same way. Specify this option and attach a copy of the late withdrawal form as soon as possible.
  • Special consideration. For students returning after three or more years who want to enter a new program, or who can show that life experience has prepared them to complete the program. Contact the graduate coordinator for that program.

Start by writing to your graduate coordinator or department chair, stating why your GPA fell below 3.00 or why you were removed from the program for a reason unrelated to grades, and how you'll improve your grades or address the performance issues and stay in good standing if reinstated. Then meet with them to discuss what's in the letter.

If the coordinator supports reinstatement, they complete and sign the Petition for Graduate Reinstatement and send it with your letter to the Director of Graduate Studies, who has final authority. A student disqualified a second time normally won't be considered.

who files

The coordinator initiates the petition, not the student. Work closely with them so the request carries correct information and so you understand both reinstatement and readmission. International students contact the Center for International Education and work with an advisor there on visa compliance.

career readiness

The Next Step: CSU Graduate Career Readiness is a free, self-paced micro-credential program open to CSU graduate students. It's built on the NACE Career Readiness Competencies and asks students to reflect on their academic and professional experience and describe it in the terms employers use. Students work at their own pace and earn eight LinkedIn-shareable competency badges, which stack into the full micro-credential.

The eight competencies are career and self development, communication, critical thinking, equity and inclusion, leadership, professionalism, teamwork, and technology. Cal State East Bay faculty contributed to module design and content development.
