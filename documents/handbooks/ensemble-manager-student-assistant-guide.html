the position

Two Ensemble Managers work with the department's ensembles. One covers the jazz and rock ensembles, the other the chamber ensemble. The work is setup before each meeting and strike afterward, the same at the ensemble's concerts and recitals, with the music equipment office, the audio technician, and the stage managers alongside you at performances.

reporting and schedules

You report to your ensemble director, who sets the call time for each meeting and the schedule for concerts and recitals. Trade or cover a call only with the director's approval, and give as much notice as you can when you're sick or can't make one.

Keys, equipment, repairs, and anything you find in a room go to the Equipment Technician in MB 1525. At performances you work with the audio technician and the stage managers, who cover audio, video, lighting, and the performance space itself.

Performance calls fall outside the weekly rehearsal pattern: evenings and occasional weekends, set as the concert calendar is set.

keys and the locker

One combination gets you into the student assistant locker, which holds the keys to the rooms and closets on your assignment. The Equipment Technician shows you the locker and gives you the combination at the start of the semester. Keep it to yourself. It doesn't go to other students, to performers, or to visitors.

The jazz and rock manager takes the keys to MB 1039 and its jazz closet, the first closet in the band room, and to MB 1527. The chamber manager takes the keys to MB 1055 and to MB 1047, where the recital hall chairs and stands live. Take only the keys your call needs, return every one of them to the locker before you leave, and report a lost key the same day.

The recital hall booth in MB 1055 has its own combination, which the audio technician holds. Lighting and audio in the hall go through the audio technician.

on the job

People will come to you with questions: an ensemble member looking for a stand, a parent at a concert, a guest artist who can't find the stage door. Answer what you know, and send anything else to the equipment office or the music office rather than guessing.

Food and drink stay out of the recital hall and the rehearsal rooms. Water in a closed bottle is fine for performers backstage. During a performance, phones are silenced.

what not to touch

Do not attempt instrument repairs, electrical work, or lamp changes on your own. Do not move equipment out of the building. Do not enter the booth or run the light board. Do not let anyone into a locked room, closet, or storage space without checking with the Equipment Technician.

federal work-study

Both positions are Federal Work-Study. Your pay comes out of a financial aid award that sets a dollar amount for the year rather than a number of hours. The full eligibility rules, the hiring steps, and the increase request are on the University's Federal Work-Study page.

your award

Your award is $3,000 for the academic year at $16.90 an hour, about 177 hours. There is no split between fall and spring: work through the award as your calls fall across the year. Federal Work-Study is not available in summer. Tell your ensemble director while you still have hours left, and the department adjusts the funding or puts in an increase request.

You can ask for more. Email the Office of Financial Aid and Scholarships at finaid@csueastbay.edu from your Horizon account with your NetID and a request to increase your annual amount. Awards move in the other direction too: an outside scholarship or other new aid can reduce a Federal Work-Study award, and money already earned above the reduced amount is repaid to the University. Check your award in MyCSUEB after any change to your aid.

staying eligible

Eligibility rests on three things: a FAFSA filed for the current year, half-time enrollment through fall and spring (6 units undergraduate, 4 units graduate), and Satisfactory Academic Progress. Dropping below half-time ends the job. Talk to your ensemble director and to Financial Aid before a schedule change that would take you under.

the 20-hour cap

Student assistants work at most 20 hours per week. The cap counts every campus job you hold. If you work somewhere else on campus, tell both supervisors and keep the combined total under 20.

Your paid time is the setup before a meeting and the strike after it. If you're enrolled in the ensemble you manage, the rehearsal itself is class time and doesn't go on the timesheet.

entering your hours

The work week runs Saturday through Friday. Hours go in through MyHR every Friday: sign in, find the Student Assistant Time header, and select the Timesheet link. Enter the hours you worked on each day, weekend calls included, and submit before you leave on Friday. Hours reported late are paid on the following cycle.

Payroll check-in happens once, at the start. Bring a state-issued ID and your Social Security card, or a US passport, birth certificate, or permanent residency card, to the Payroll Office on the second floor of the Student Services Administration building. Direct deposit is set up in the same visit.

tracking your balance

The award and the rate are filled in. Enter the hours you report each week and the sheet keeps the running balance. It prints to PDF for your own records or for a check-in with your director. What you type stays in this browser on this device.

the weekly round

the setup plan

Each director sets the room for their own ensemble: the chair and stand arrangement, the amps, the kit and backline, the piano position, the risers, and where the percussion goes. Get that plan from your director at the start of the semester and keep a copy with you. When a rehearsal calls for a change to it, the director tells you ahead of the call.

Anything the plan calls for that you can't find, or that needs a key you don't hold, goes to the Equipment Technician before the meeting rather than at the door.

setup

  1. Take the keys your call needs from the student assistant locker.
  2. Unlock the room and turn on the lights.
  3. Set the room from the director's plan.
  4. Check what you set out: amps on and working, cables intact, stands and chairs sound, the kit complete, the piano bench in place.
  5. Report anything broken or missing to the Equipment Technician, and tell the director what you're short.
  6. Be in the room and ready at the start of the meeting.

strike

Nothing stays out after your call. Everything goes back to the closet, cabinet, or shelf it came from.

  1. Strike the room and reset it for the next class or rehearsal in it.
  2. Chairs stacked and stands collapsed, back where they came from.
  3. Amps off, cables coiled and banded, the kit and the keyboards back in place.
  4. Piano fallboard closed and locked.
  5. Lights off, closets and the room locked.
  6. Return every key to the student assistant locker.
what you leave behind

Take anything a member leaves in the room to the equipment office in MB 1525 with a note saying where you found it.

concerts and recitals

who does what

The stage managers set the performance space: chairs, stands, risers, piano position, the marquee, the door during the performance, and the strike. The audio technician runs the microphones, the recording, the video, and the lighting. Your part is the ensemble's own setup and gear: what the ensemble plays on and what it needs on stage.

The setup chart comes from your director. Get it before setup day and take it to the stage managers and the audio technician so all three of you are working from the same stage.

before the performance

  1. Move the ensemble's instruments, amps, and gear to the venue on the schedule your director sets.
  2. Set the ensemble's places from the chart, alongside the stage managers.
  3. Tell the audio technician what needs a microphone or a line, and where.
  4. Stay for the sound check and the warm-up, and handle what the director asks for on stage.
  5. Clear cases, covers, and spare gear off the stage and out of the wings before the house opens.

during the performance

Stay where the director can reach you, on stage side. Handle setup changes between pieces that involve the ensemble's own equipment, and leave the house, the door, and the booth to the stage managers and the audio technician.

after the performance

  1. Wait for the audio technician to stop the recording before anything moves.
  2. Strike the ensemble's gear and take it back to its room and closet.
  3. Help the stage managers reset the venue for the next day.
  4. Lock the closets and the rooms you opened, and return your keys to the locker.

reporting problems

Watch for burned-out lights, leaks, damaged doors and locks, broken chairs and stands, torn cables, and pianos that have been moved or damaged. Report what you find to the Equipment Technician, who files the work order with Facilities. Do not attempt the repair yourself and do not file work orders under your own name.

Report anything missing the moment you notice it: an instrument, an amp, a microphone, a cable. Send the message that day rather than at the end of the week, and copy your director.

For an emergency, injury, or anyone in the building who should not be there, call University Police at 510-885-3333 first, then notify the Equipment Technician.
