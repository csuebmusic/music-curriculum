Equipment Office Student Assistant Guide

Audio Technician and Stage Manager positions, Music Equipment Office (MB 1525)
document: student assistant guide unit: music equipment office effective: fall 2026 status: draft

the positions

Three student assistants work out of the music equipment office: one audio technician and two stage managers. The work covers the daily rhythm of the building, opening and closing the practice rooms, keeping instruments and equipment in their places, and running the setup, recording, and strike for department performances.

audio technician and stage managers

All three positions share the daily rounds in sections 03 through 05, and all three work performances.

The audio technician runs the recording station and the video camera, sets levels, handles microphones, cables, and stands, and delivers the finished files to the equipment office for cataloging. On jazz events, the audio technician works with the ensemble director on the audio setup. All audio and video gear is the audio technician's to keep organized and in place.

The stage managers set up and reset the performance space: chairs, stands, risers, piano position, lighting, the marquee and programs, door coverage during the performance, and the strike afterward. On larger events the two split front of house and stage. Outside performances, chairs, stands, percussion, and the pianos are theirs to put away and keep in order.

reporting and schedules

You report to the Equipment Technician in MB 1525. Shifts are set at the start of each semester around your class schedule. Trade or cover a shift only with the Equipment Technician's approval, and give as much notice as you can when you are sick or cannot make a shift.

Performance shifts fall outside the regular weekday pattern: evenings and occasional weekends, posted as the concert calendar is set. Hours, the weekly cap, and the timesheet are covered in section 02.

keys, codes, and accounts

Two combinations get you through the building: one for the recital hall booth and one for the student assistant locker, which holds the keys to the rooms and closets on your round. Both stay with you. They do not go to other students, to performers, or to visitors, and they are not written down in this guide.

Take only the keys your shift needs and return every one of them to the locker before you leave. Report a lost key the same day.

on the job

You are the department's face for anyone who walks in: students looking for a room, a parent at a recital, a guest artist who cannot find the stage door. Answer what you know, and send anything else to the equipment office or the music office rather than guessing.

Food and drink stay out of the recital hall, the practice rooms, and the studios. Water in a closed bottle is fine for performers backstage. During a performance, phones are silenced and stay away from the booth and the stage.

what not to touch

Do not attempt instrument repairs, electrical work, or lamp changes on your own. Do not move equipment out of the building. Do not let anyone into a locked room, studio, or storage space without checking with the Equipment Technician.

federal work-study

All three positions are Federal Work-Study. Your pay comes out of a financial aid award that sets a dollar amount for the year rather than a number of hours. The full eligibility rules, the hiring steps, and the increase request are on the University's Federal Work-Study page.

your award

Most awards are $3,000 for the academic year, and the amount is a ceiling on what you can earn in the position. It covers fall and spring; Federal Work-Study is not available in summer. Track the balance as you go and tell the Equipment Technician before you reach it.

You can ask for more. Email the Office of Financial Aid and Scholarships at finaid@csueastbay.edu from your Horizon account with your NetID and a request to increase your annual amount. Awards move in the other direction too: an outside scholarship or other new aid can reduce a Federal Work-Study award, and money already earned above the reduced amount is repaid to the University. Check your award in MyCSUEB after any change to your aid.

staying eligible

Eligibility rests on three things: a FAFSA filed for the current year, half-time enrollment through fall and spring (6 units undergraduate, 4 units graduate), and Satisfactory Academic Progress. Dropping below half-time ends the job. Talk to the Equipment Technician and to Financial Aid before a schedule change that would take you under.

the 20-hour cap

Student assistants work at most 20 hours per week. The cap counts every campus job you hold, so tell both supervisors if you work somewhere else on campus and keep the combined total under it. Shifts are built around your class times, and you do not work during a class you are enrolled in.

entering your hours

Hours go in through MyHR every Friday. Sign in, find the Student Assistant Time header, and select the Timesheet link. Enter the hours you worked on each day of the week and submit before you leave on Friday. Hours reported late are paid on the following cycle.

Payroll check-in happens once, at the start. Bring a state-issued ID and your Social Security card, or a US passport, birth certificate, or permanent residency card, to the Payroll Office on the second floor of the Student Services Administration building. Direct deposit is set up in the same visit.

tracking your balance

Enter your award, your hourly rate, and the hours you report each week. The sheet keeps the running balance and prints to PDF for your own records or for a check-in with the Equipment Technician. What you type stays in this browser on this device.

opening the practice rooms

Practice rooms are unlocked from 8:30 AM to 6:00 PM, Monday through Friday, for music majors and minors and for approved non-majors in select music courses. Access outside those hours runs through the equipment office by request, at least 36 hours ahead.

the opening round

  1. Start the round at 8:15 AM so every room is open by 8:30. First floor, then second.
  2. Unlock each room and prop the door until you have checked it.
  3. Look at the piano: fallboard unlocked, bench in place and intact, nothing left on the lid.
  4. Clear anything left overnight. Personal items go to the equipment office.
  5. Note anything broken, missing, or leaking and report it.
safety

Practice room door windows stay uncovered at all times. If you find one papered over, take the covering down.

closing the practice rooms

the closing round

Begin the closing round at 6:00 PM.

  1. Knock before entering. A room with someone practicing in it stays open: tell them the round is closing and ask them to shut the door firmly on their way out.
  2. Close and lock the piano fallboard. Return the bench, chair, and stand to their places.
  3. Turn off the lights.
  4. Lock the door and check that it latched.
  5. Take anything left behind to the equipment office and log it.

reserved rooms

Students may reserve a practice room past 6:00 PM through the equipment office. A reserved room carries a posted sign with the student's name, the room number, and the hours. Leave that room available: set the lock and leave the door ajar. The student holding the reservation secures the door when they finish.

If a reservation is posted for a room and nobody is in it by the end of your round, leave it as posted. Send anyone who wants a room for a later evening to the equipment office to arrange it.

The stage managers check that the pianos in the recital hall, the band room, and the classrooms are locked at the end of the day, and that the practice room pianos are in working order. Anything past that, a sticking key, a broken bench, a piano that has been moved, goes to the Equipment Technician.

The rooms to walk at the end of the day are MB 1039 and its three closets, MB 1047 and the percussion cave, MB 1055, MB 1058, MB 1527, and any classroom that ran a methods or ensemble session that day.

When an instrument comes back to you, look it over before it goes on the shelf: case latches, loose screws, missing pads, damage. Set anything that needs attention aside for the Equipment Technician with a note, rather than shelving it.

checkout counter

Checkouts and locker assignments are handled by the Equipment Technician. Send students who come in to borrow an instrument, return one, or claim a locker to MB 1525, and tell them the office hours if the technician is out. What you can do at the counter is take a message, take in a returned instrument and set it aside with a note, and answer questions about hours and locations.

Equipment is due the last day of finals, lockers are renewed at the end of each semester, and a lost locker key carries a $20 replacement charge. Anything past that goes to the technician.

concerts and recitals

the two venues

MB 1055 is the recital hall, used for student recitals, small groups, and guest artists. MB 1039 is the main rehearsal room and hosts smaller ensembles, jazz recitals, and the larger ensembles when they perform.

Scheduling, programs, and the concert calendar run through the Music Resource Center. Setup charts come from the ensemble director. Get the chart before setup day and ask about anything on it you do not recognize.

before the performance

  1. Set the stage from the director's chart: chairs, stands, risers, piano position, music stands for the conductor and soloists.
  2. Look over the seating area, the lobby, and the green room. If they are not clean, contact custodial services.
  3. Put the wooden marquee outside and post the performance on it.
  4. Programs are posted by QR code in the hall. Print a few paper copies for the sound and light operator.
  5. Turn on and check the recording station and the video camera. See section 06.
  6. Turn the work lights off no later than one hour before the performance and bring up the board for stage and hall lighting.
  7. Open the house at the time the director sets, usually thirty minutes before curtain.

during the performance

One stage manager stays at the door to greet patrons, seat latecomers between pieces, and keep food and drink out of the hall. Patrons wait outside, performers wait in the hallway or the green room. The other stage manager stays on stage side for setup changes between pieces and for anything the performers need.

after the performance

  1. Stop and save the recording and the video before anything else moves.
  2. Strike the stage and reset the room for the next day's classes or rehearsals.
  3. Bring the marquee back inside.
  4. Return borrowed equipment, cables, and microphones to MB 1525.
  5. Work lights on, board down, booth locked, hall locked.
  6. Hand the audio and video files to the equipment office for editing, cataloging, and delivery to the performers.

jazz concerts and the festival

Jazz events are the heaviest setups in the department: microphones, monitors, the cable snake, and a full audio patch. Plan on a couple of hours with more than one person on it. The setup book in the equipment office has the reference charts, and the jazz director provides the chart for the specific group.

A jazz concert is a single day: setup in the morning, performance at night. The Jazz Festival runs two to three days in April with visiting schools, and adds two warm-up rooms set with chairs, stands, and amps. Festival preparation starts in January.

audio, video, and lighting

recording station

The recording station and the light board have their own handout, which covers the booth in technical detail. The recording station in the MB 1055 booth comes up before every performance. Power it on early, check levels during the sound check, and confirm you are recording before the first piece. Audio and video equipment is stored in MB 1058.

video

The video camera is set up ahead of the performance and started at least five minutes before the downbeat. It records to a 128 GB SD card, which you download to the equipment office after the performance. Editing is handled by the Music Resource Center.

lighting board

The board is in the MB 1055 booth, and its operating instructions sit next to it. Each fader is a submaster and is labeled with its function. Work lights go off at least an hour before the performance. The overhead stage light stays on whenever the hall is in use, unless a performer or instructor asks for it down, which happens most often for a visual presentation. Report burned-out fixtures to the Equipment Technician.

before you mix

If a performance calls for live mixing beyond your experience, say so in advance. The department brings in an experienced operator for the larger jazz events.

reporting problems

On your daily rounds, watch for burned-out lights, leaks, damaged doors and locks, broken chairs and stands, and pianos that have been moved or damaged. Report what you find to the Equipment Technician, who files the work order with Facilities. Do not attempt the repair yourself and do not file work orders under your own name.

Report anything that is missing the moment you notice it. An instrument, microphone, or cable that is not where it should be is worth a message that day rather than at the end of the week.

For an emergency, injury, or anyone in the building who should not be there, call University Police at 510-885-3333 first, then notify the Equipment Technician.
